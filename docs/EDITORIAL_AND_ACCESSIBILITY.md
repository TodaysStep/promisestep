# PromiseStep — editorial and accessibility standard

This is the existing visual language, not a rebrand. The approved method is **Refactoring for Kindness, Familiarity, and Visual Metaphor**. Maintain the distinct meaning of each principle in images, headings, explanations and tested interactions.

## Visual grammar

**Type.** Quiet editorial serif for core ideas; unobtrusive readable interface text for instructions and results; short headings that carry meaning without decorative jargon.

**Color and material.** Warm paper, natural dark ink, soft botanical greens, restrained amber/translucent glass and original calligraphic gestures. Contrast must remain readable over imagery on mobile and desktop.

**Images.** Use the sixteen original text-free scene photographs, existing authored SVG illustrations and the three separate instrument objects. No emoji, stock substitutes, unrelated portraits, invented visual identities or November 15 campaign art in the website release. Do not burn navigation, captions or calls to action into images.

**Motion.** Editorial pace. The carousel is under visitor control, with meaningful pause and previous/next buttons, no input loss, no focus traps and no unexpected movement while someone is reading.

## Interaction contracts

- Every field has a visible label and an accessible name. The correction reason is user-authored, required and preserved through export and restoration.
- Invalid input refuses **before mutation**; keep the typed draft, explain the problem, mark the field and focus the relevant control.
- An asynchronous result must not steal focus from later typing. Busy states must be perceivable and recoverable.
- Export, Restore and Clear must clearly describe the effect; a conflicting archive cannot silently overwrite a newer correction; Clear is deliberate and protects the last copy.
- Follow mobile 320–440 px reflow without horizontal overflow. Do not assume a desktop-only hover interaction.
- Information hierarchy must distinguish authored facts, suggestions, verification scope, refusal, and planned capabilities.

## Content integrity

“Working” means verified implemented behavior, not intent. “Planned” means proposed direction, not a deployed endpoint. “Reviewed” requires an independent review receipt; a tool read or a source test does not count. HSG I–V are ratified; later volumes are not.

**Promises need accurate standing.** Keep proven claims close to executable evidence, caveats near the relevant claim, and longer provenance in linked technical notes rather than a wall of release statuses on the homepage.

## Review questions

Can a first-time visitor find the next action? Does a returning visitor recognize where to resume? Can a keyboard-only reader perform the entire lab? Are the original and correction separately visible? Does mobile look intentional, not merely squeezed? Does the page communicate refusal without alarm or blame? Are downloads and their limits understandable?

Offline layout tests are informative but not hosted Safari acceptance. Maintain that distinction.
